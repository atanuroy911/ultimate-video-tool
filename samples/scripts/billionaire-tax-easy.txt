Why do billionaires pay less tax than you?

Meet Jack. Jack earns one hundred thousand dollars a year. Every month he gets paid. Every month he pays tax.

Now meet Mr. Rich. Mr. Rich owns a company worth ten billion dollars.

Some years, Mr. Rich pays less income tax than Jack. And it is legal. How?

Here is the secret. Jack gets a paycheck. Mr. Rich gets richer.

Mr. Rich owns shares. One share is worth one hundred dollars. A year later, it is worth two hundred. He is richer. But he sold nothing. So there is no paycheck. And no paycheck means no income tax.

But then how does he buy a mansion?

He goes to a bank. He says: lend me twenty million dollars. If I don't pay you back, you can keep some of my shares.

The bank says yes. He buys the mansion. And a loan is not income. So no tax.

Of course, he must pay the loan back, with interest. But he can pay it with another loan.

Does anyone really do this? In 2021, reporters at ProPublica looked at the tax records of the richest Americans. Jeff Bezos paid no federal income tax in 2007 and 2011. Elon Musk paid none in 2018.

So what happens when Mr. Rich finally sells? He pays tax on his profit. So he waits. And waits.

When he dies, his children get the shares. And the price the tax counts from resets to today's price. Years of profit are never taxed.

People call it: buy, borrow, die.

Why can't Jack do it? Because Jack needs his paycheck to pay rent. Mr. Rich can wait.

The tax system does not tax how rich you are. It taxes how money reaches you.

So here is the real question. If owning things is so powerful, why were we only taught to earn a salary?
