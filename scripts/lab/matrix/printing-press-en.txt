In the fourteen hundreds, books were copied by hand. A single Bible could take a monk three years, so only churches and kings owned books. Then, around the year fourteen forty, a goldsmith named Johannes Gutenberg built a press with movable metal letters. Suddenly a shop could print hundreds of pages in a day. Books became cheap. Ideas spread across Europe in weeks instead of years. Within fifty years, millions of books existed, and more people learned to read than ever before.
